## Proteins in motion

Protein function depends on more than a single structure. Hydrogen–deuterium exchange mass spectrometry, or HDX-MS, measures changes in deuterium uptake over time and helps researchers investigate protein dynamics and differences between experimental states.

The resulting data have several dimensions: sequence position, exchange time, biological state, peptide charge and replicates. Analysing them together requires careful aggregation, quality control and an explicit treatment of uncertainty.

## Questions we explore

- How can complex measurements be summarised without losing their experimental context?
- Which differences between protein states are supported by the data and their uncertainty?
- What additional information can overlapping peptides provide about local protein dynamics?

## Our approach

We develop tools that connect statistical analysis with visual exploration. HaDeX2 supports multidimensional analysis and visualisation of HDX-MS data, helping researchers examine coverage, measurement variability and differences between experimental conditions.

Our high-resolution work addresses the relationship between peptide measurements and more local estimates of deuterium uptake. HRaDeX explores residue-level uptake rates through kinetic modelling. These approaches provide complementary ways to interrogate the data, with the underlying assumptions remaining important to interpretation.
